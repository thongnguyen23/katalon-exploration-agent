### <a id="concept-8842" class="anchor_top_offset"/>Choose a mobile device

<p xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" className="shortdesc">Testing on an Android device like a Pixel 5  will not necessarily tell you whether your application works on a Samsung G7, let alone whether it will work on an iPhone. How many OS and device types you test on is defined as your device coverage, and the more devices, the better the coverage. Improving device coverage and choosing the right devices, however, creates challenges, such as:</p> 
<div xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" className="p"><ol className="ol"><li className="li">
      <p className="p"><strong className="ph b">Device fragmentation:</strong> The multitude of mobile devices presents a challenge for software developers and QA testers who need to ensure their application functions correctly across the varied devices their audience uses. This is especially pronounced in the Android ecosystem, which is notorious for its extensive fragmentation, featuring thousands of devices with unique specifications.</p>
    </li><li className="li">
      <p className="p"><strong className="ph b">Budget constraints:</strong> Establishing and maintaining a comprehensive on-site device lab for testing can be expensive. This poses a significant challenge for startups and smaller development teams operating under tight budget constraints.</p>
    </li><li className="li">
      <p className="p"><strong className="ph b">Time constraints:</strong> Conducting thorough tests across numerous devices is time-intensive. Developers and QA teams often face the added pressure of needing to expedite app releases, which can compromise the depth and scope of testing.</p>
    </li><li className="li">
      <p className="p"><strong className="ph b">Limited resources:</strong> Smaller teams may lack the necessary manpower to extensively test an app on a broad array of devices, limiting the thoroughness of their testing efforts.</p>
    </li></ol></div>
<div xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml" className="p">To overcome these challenges, consider the following strategies for selecting which devices to test your mobile application on:<table className="simpletable"><colgroup><col style={{width: '50%'}} /><col style={{width: '50%'}} /></colgroup><thead><tr className="sthead"><th className="stentry" scope="col">Challenge</th><th className="stentry" scope="col">Strategy</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr className="strow"><td className="stentry">Device fragmentation</td><td className="stentry">Analyze your user data and demographics to determine which devices are popular among your target market. You can use tools as simple as a customer survey or a complete suite Google Analytics to identify what devices your customers are using.</td></tr><tr className="strow"><td className="stentry">Budget constraints</td><td className="stentry">Review your market share data for various devices and operating systems to determine which devices would return the most business value. For example, you may want to focus on just iOS devices first.</td></tr><tr className="strow"><td className="stentry">Time constraints</td><td className="stentry">To overcome budget constraints, consider utilizing cloud-based device testing services, which provide access to a wide range of devices at a lower cost compared to maintaining an on-site device lab. Katalon Studio connects to TestCloud natively for this.</td></tr><tr className="strow"><td className="stentry">Limited resources</td><td className="stentry">Choose a good device mix ratio. When selecting devices for manual testing, aim to include a high-end model, a low-end model, and a tablet if applicable. For Android, start with popular brands to ensure coverage across different price points and access to the latest Android versions. For automated testing, apply the same device strategy but expand the variety of OS versions to enhance coverage and speed up test execution.</td></tr></tbody></table>When you are done deciding on the types of devices you will be using for your mobile testing in Katalon Studio, proceed to the first step in the mobile testing workflow: <a className="xref" href="/katalon-studio/cookbooks/mobile-testing-cookbook/the-mobile-testing-cookbook#task-352">Create a test project</a>.</div>
